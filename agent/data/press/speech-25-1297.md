We have just concluded our fourth European Humanitarian Forum. I would like to thank the Polish
Presidency for their outstanding cooperation in co-hosting this event.
It is only when we are tested that we find out who we truly are and what we truly believe. Today our
humanitarian system is being tested. This Forum is our collective answer to this test.
It shows who we are and what we — as a humanitarian community — truly believe. Saving lives,
easing the suffering, and cooperating with partners to help those most in need – no matter where
they are on this planet.
The European Union will continue to be a strong and reliable humanitarian partner. We are not
stepping back. In fact, we are doing the opposite. We are increasing our support to people in need.
We are becoming the world's number one provider of humanitarian assistance.
This year we have increased our initial humanitarian aid budget to 1.9 billion euro, higher than any
previous year. We have reinforced this with an additional €350 million in April, for a total of almost
€2.3 billion.
This funding supports the most pressing global crises and strengthens the core humanitarian services,
such as logistics, security and humanitarian flights, hit hard by the recent funding cuts.
Today over 305 million people need humanitarian assistance. Needs are rising, funding is being cut,
access is blocked, humanitarian law is violated, and aid workers are being targeted and killed. This is
our world today.
More and more, we hear the question: “What's in it for me?” This is the wrong way to go. Global
solidarity and the rules based international order are the cornerstones of global peace, security, and
prosperity. That is why the European Union is taking a different path. We will continue to be a strong,
reliable, and principled global partner.
Humanity, neutrality, impartiality, independence — these are the guiding lights of our humanitarian
action. At this Humanitarian Forum, partners from the four corners of the globe came to show that we
are fighting back for the people we serve. We brought together UN agencies, NGOs, local
humanitarian workers, international organisations, donors, multilateral development banks, the
private sector, volunteers, think tanks and decision-makers, over 2,300 global participants.
At this year's Forum, we focused on three crucial areas.
First, rethinking how we do humanitarian aid in this new reality — to make it more efficient, more
accountable, and more principled.
Second, strengthening humanitarian diplomacy to uphold international humanitarian law and to
protect civilians,  especially the most vulnerable.
Third, developing a comprehensive and integrated EU approach to tackling fragility across the world.
Humanitarian action, development cooperation, and peacebuilding — all working together.
Let me be clear on one thing: as humanitarians, we are not in the business of doing deals or
weaponizing or politicising humanitarian aid. We believe in global cooperation to save lives. Full stop.
We act on principles, not politics. Anywhere there is need, we are there.
During these two days, we also focused on urgent crises. The EU reaffirmed its full support for the
people of Ukraine. Our long-term commitment is stronger than ever. We called for all civilians to be
protected and for full humanitarian access.
We also discussed the nightmare unfolding in Gaza. Over two months of blockade. No food, no
medicine, people are starving. We call for the protection of all civilians and aid workers. Immediate
humanitarian access must be granted to legitimate humanitarian partners to provide aid in line with
humanitarian principles. The EU is providing an additional €50 million in humanitarian funding for the
people of Gaza and the West Bank. International Humanitarian Law must be respected.
Sudan was also at the top of our agenda. It is the world's largest displacement crisis — over 11
million displaced and over 3 million refugees. We must do more to address the impact of this
devastating war.
We have not forgotten other global crises. After my trip to Syria, we have decided to provide an
additional €20 million in humanitarian aid to Syria. We are also allocating an additional €13 million for
Lebanon.
We also discussed the best way to move forward as a humanitarian community. The EU will stay fully
committed to effective multilateralism and to the rules-based order, with the United Nations at its
core. We will do our part to support the UN reforms, including the Humanitarian Reset and the UN80
initiative, to become more effective, cost-efficient, and responsive.
The EU is committed to developing a new integrated approach to fragility by next year to address the
needs of the most vulnerable, by connecting urgent relief with longer-term solutions. Humanitarian,
development, and peace efforts — together. But we cannot do it alone. We need all our international
partners with us.
This Forum is about our humanity, our solidarity, and our responsibility to act. The European Union
will live up to its responsibility.
